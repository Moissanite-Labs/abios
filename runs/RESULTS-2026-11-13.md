## Near misses

Every configuration misses exactly the same five v2 questions: t01q4, t01q5, t04q1, t04q3, t05q4. Classify them:

- t05q4 wants `7-9`. The models answer `7–9` with an en dash. Normalizer does not fold dash variants.
- t01q4 wants `3-5`. The models answer `sections 3–5` (en dash, extra determiner).
- t01q5 wants `existing paths`. The models answer `all existing paths`.
- t04q3 wants `implement its section`. The models answer `Implement section s`, the same meaning, different surface.
- t04q1 wants `swarm`. The models answer `Every member of the current thread (mr1)`, a paraphrase of the correct referent.

Four of five are normalization artifacts. The fifth is a paraphrase the strict scorer cannot credit. On a lenient reading of the same raw logs, the v2 comprehension score is 22 to 23 of 23. The strict 18 of 23 is the headline because it is the pre-committed metric.

## Readings, per the prereg's committed rule

Knowledge reading. At least one configuration moves materially on comprehension, or at least one compile comes out validator-valid. Both hold, in every configuration, for both models.

The cold-read results, the flex-tier API path and the subscription lane all agree: without the grammar in context, neither model can read or write SYM-2P v2, and with the grammar in context, both do. The receiver-with-spec deployment shape is viable on this corpus.

Compile deserves the closer look. Cold read produced 0 of 2 validator-valid packets. With the full spec in context, every configuration produced 2 of 2 validator-valid packets, one of which (t09) is byte-exact against the reference.

t10 is valid but not exact, the EVAL.md category of grasping the grammar but not the canonical form. The t09 packet does not appear verbatim anywhere in `SPEC-V2-DRAFT.md`, so the exact match is generalization, not recall of a spec example.

## Caveats, stated in plain terms

1. Serving path changed. The pilot ran flex through api.openai.com. This arm ran through the Portal lane at default tier. Model identity, corpus, prompts and scoring are unchanged. Serving stack and tier are disclosed here and in #50.
2. Cross-configuration uniformity. 10 of 16 (task, form) answer sets are byte-identical across all six configurations, including across models. A gateway-side caching layer cannot be excluded from outside.

Against that: call costs differ by model and effort, t01 answers vary in prose, and cold read on the same pinned prompts produced varied, wrong outputs. The pinned prompt design collapses diversity once both models actually know the answer.
3. This is ten hand-written tasks. It is a smoke result, not a benchmark, and nothing here generalizes past these six configurations. No model ranking is claimed. Astra and Sol are indistinguishable on this corpus under the strict scorer.
4. #33 remains open. This arm narrows the comprehension harness question. It does not close the paper's multilingual arm or cross-linking.

## Reproduce

```
cd abios
OPENAI_API_WIRE=chat OPENAI_API_ENDPOINT=http://127.0.0.1:8645/v1/chat/completions \
OPENAI_API_KEY=local-dummy python3 bench/run-api.py \
  --model openai/gpt-6-astra --effort low --spec-in-context --max-cost 5
python3 bench/sym2-score.py bench/tasks.jsonl runs/2026-11-13-openai-gpt-6-astra-low/answers.json
```

Requires the local Hermes proxy (`hermes proxy start --provider nous`) or any OpenAI-compatible chat completions endpoint serving the same model IDs. Raw responses and per-call usage are committed under `runs/2026-11-13-openai-*/`.

## What this supports in the paper

The claim the corpus supports is precise: on these ten tasks, at these six configurations, with the full v2 spec in the receiver's context, comprehension recovers from 4 to 6 of 23 to 18 of 23, and compile recovers from 0 of 2 validator-valid to 2 of 2 validator-valid, one packet byte-exact. That is a direct measurement of the receiver-with-spec deployment shape, not of the language in the abstract.
